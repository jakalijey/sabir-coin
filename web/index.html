Why the name

Sabır

Turkish for patience. Said sah-bur. The dotless ı is a short, soft vowel, so the ticker is SABIR.

Sabrın sonu selamettir."The end of patience is safety." A Turkish saying. In SABIR, the end of patience is the low exit tax and your share of the vault.
Sabreden derviş muradına ermiş."The dervish who waited got his wish." In SABIR, the one who waits five minutes walks through the door for free.

Every Turkish grandmother says these. SABIR puts them in a contract, where nobody can talk their way around them.

The door keeps a record

Every entry is written down. Paying is allowed, and everyone gets to see how long you lasted.

Wall of Impatience

    Wall of Patience

      Three rules, all in the contract

      No owner, no admin method, no off-chain server deciding anything. The numbers are fixed at launch and anyone can read them.

      1

      The Gate

      Everyone pays a small fee and starts a five-minute wait. Walking through early costs a toll that starts at 5 NEAR and melts to zero. Earlier entrants get a larger grant, so waiting has a price too.

      2

      The Vault

      Every fee and toll goes into a vault that backs the supply. You can always burn SABIR for your share of it. There is no pool to drain and nobody who can move the money.

      3

      The Exit

      Leaving or sending costs up to 50% tax, shrinking to 5% after 30 days of holding. Taxed value stays behind, so every early exit raises the backing of those who stayed.

      What happens over 60 days

      The contract's formulas run in a simulation before launch, as the parameters are final once deployed. The line is the NEAR backing 100,000 SABIR.

      What this does not solve

      Written plainly, because the mechanic should be judged on what it really does.

      • Sybil: one person can open many accounts. Each pays the fee and the anti-profit rule stops instant gains, so farming loses money in the simulation. It can still fill the gate, which is griefing, and the limit is its cost.
      • Patience is measured in holding time, not character. A wallet that forgets its keys for 30 days looks like a saint.
      • Late entrants receive smaller grants and can lose money if nobody comes after them. The gains of those who stay come from those who leave and those who pay. This is a game with winners and losers, not a yield product.
      • No DEX pool in v1. A pool would pay the tax on every trade, so the vault is the only exit.
      • This deployment runs on NEAR testnet with no real value.
      • The clock uses block time, and a transfer blends the receiver's clock by weight. Small top-ups barely hurt a patient holder, by design.
